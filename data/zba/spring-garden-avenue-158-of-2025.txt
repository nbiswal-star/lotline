Findings of Fact: 
1. The Subject Property is located at 1065 Spring Garden Avenue in an R1A -H 
(Residential One Unit Attached High Density) District in Spring Garden. 
2. The parcel has a triangular shape and an area of 3,550 sf.  Its 50’ width on Spring 
Garden Avenue tapers to 15’ on Wesler Way at the rear.   
3. The only structure on the property was condemned and has been demolished. That 
structure was set back approximately 10’ from the front property line.   
4. The house on the adjacent property located at 1063 Spring Garden Avenue fronts 
onto Wesler Way.

5. The Subject Property is at a bend on Spring Garden Avenue and a  significant, 
undeveloped hill side is located across the street. 
6. A variety of residential and non-residential uses are located within the general 
vicinity of the Subject Property. 
 
7. The Applicant demolished the existing structure on the property and proposes to 
construct a new three-story structure that would be set back 16’-3” from Spring Garden Avenue, 
5’ from the property line shared with the parcel located at 1103 Spring Garden Avenue, 0’ from 
the property line shared with 1063 Spring Garden Avenue; and 35’ from the limited rear property 
line on Wesler Way.   
8. With the exception of the 0’ setback on the side shared with rear yard of 1063 Spring 
Garden Avenue, the proposed structure would comply with the site development standards for 
R1A-H Districts. 
9. The Applicant proposes to use the new structure for two residential units, with a unit 
on the first and second floors and a unit on the third floor. 
10. A two-car parking pad with access from Wesler Way is proposed for the rear of the 
site. 
11. The Applicant asserted that it would not be economically feasible to construct a 
house for a single residential unit under current market conditions in the area. 
12. The Applicant explained that the shape of the parcel is a unique condition that 
precludes strict compliance with the Code’s setback requirements  and limits the feasibility of 
subdividing the parcel into two lots that would allow for the construction of single-family houses.  
13. The Applicant asserted that the proposed side setback would be generally 
consistent with the demolished structure, which was set back 1.5’ from the property line shared 
with Parcel No. 24 -F-197.  The front se tback would be increased from 10’ for the demolished 
structure to 16’, with the effect that the new structure would improve visibility on the bend of Spring 
Garden Avenue. 
14. The Applicant identified number of properties with multiple residential units locate d 
in the immediate vicinity of the Subject Property. 
15. The City of Pittsburgh’s 1997 Zoning Map indicates that the zoning district 
designation for the Subject Property was, at least as of 1997, R2 (Residential Two Unit), where a 
two-unit residential use would have been permitted. 
16. Erik Kath, the President of the Community Alliance of Spring Garden East 
Deutschtown, appeared at the hearing to support the request. 
17. No one appeared at the hearing to oppose the request. 
Conclusions of Law: 
1. The Applicant seeks a use variance from Section 911.02 and a dimensional 
variance from the interior side setback standard in Section 903.03.D to allow the construction of 
 
3 
 
a two-unit residential structure on the Subject Property with a 0’ interior side setback on one 
side.   
2. Section 922.09.E sets forth the general conditions the Board is to consider with 
respect to variances.  The criteria for determining whether to grant a variance include: 1) 
whether unique circumstances or conditions of a property would result in an unnecessary 
hardship; 2) whether the property could be developed in accordance with the Code’s 
requirements to allow for its reasonable use; 3) whether the applicant created the hardship; 4) 
whether the requested variance would adversely affect the essential character of the 
neighborhood or the public welfare; and 5) whether the variance requested is the minimum 
variance that would afford relief with the least modification possible.  See Marshall v. City of 
Philadelphia and Zoning Bd. of Adj., 97 A.3d 323, 329 (Pa. 2014); Hertzberg v. Zoning Board of 
Adj. of the City of Pittsburgh, 721 A.2d 43 (Pa. 1998), citing Allegheny West Civic Council v. 
Zoning Bd. of Adj. of the City of Pittsburgh, 689 A.2d 225 (Pa. 1997); see also Metal Green Inc. 
v. City of Philadelphia, 266 A.3d 495, 510 (Pa. 2021). 
3. In Marshall, the Court recognized that an unnecessary hardship can be 
established with evidence that the physical features of the property preclude its use for a 
permitted purpose; or that the property can be conformed for a permitted use only at a 
prohibitive expense.  Marshall, 97 A.3d at 333, citing O'Neill v. Philadelphia Zoning Bd. of Adj., 
120 A.2d 901 (1956).   
4. The minimum lot size in R1A Districts is 1,200 sf, and the 3,550 sf area of the 
Subject Property is sufficient for two lots that would comply with this Code requirement.  
However, the Applicant presented credible evidence that the unique triangular shape of the 
parcel would not allow for a feasible subdivision. 
5. The Board concludes that the Applicant presented sufficient evidence that, if the 
parcel could be subdivided, it could be used for two residential units.  Because subdivision is not 
feasible, the use of the single parcel for two residential units would not have a significant impact 
on the surrounding area. 
6. The Applicant also presented credible evidence that the unique shape of the 
parcel precludes strict compliance with the Code’s interior side setback requirement and that a 
reduced setback will not have any impact on the adjacent parcel. 
7. Consistent with the evidence and testimony presented, and the applicable legal 
standards governing variances, the Board concludes that approval of the request for both a use 
variance and a limited dimensional variance is appropriate. 
 
 
 
 
 
4 
 
Decision: The Applicant’s request for variances from Sections 911.02 and 903.03.D to 
allow for two residential unit use within a structure with a 0’ interior side 
setback is hereby APPROVED.
