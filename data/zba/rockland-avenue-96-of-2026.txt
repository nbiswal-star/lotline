Findings of Fact:
1. The Subject Property is a vacant, unaddressed parcel, identified as Parcel No. 35-
D-22, in an R1D-H (Residential One Unit Detached High Density) District in Beechview.
2. The parcel is a corner parcel at the intersection of Rockland Avenue and Sebring
Avenue and its dimensions are 30’ by 100’ (3,000 sf).
3. The site slopes downward from the front property line on Rockland Avenue towards
the rear property line which abuts the side of a parcel with frontage on Sebring Avenue .  A curb
cut, with access on Sebring Avenue, is located at the rear of the parcel.
4. The Applicant proposes to build a structure to be used for two  residential units on
the Subject Property.

2

5. As proposed, the new structure  would be set back 15’ from Rockland Avenue, 5’
from Sebring Avenue, 5’ from the property line shared with the parcel at 1327 Rockland Avenue,
and 25’ from the rear property line.
6. A two-car parking pad is proposed in the rear yard, with access from the existing
curb cut on Sebring Avenue.
7. The houses on Sebring Avenue, adjacent to the curb cut on the Subject Property,
have front yard setbacks that are less than 15’.
8. The Applicant asserted that the width and topography of the Subject Property are
unique characteristics that preclude strict compliance with the Code’s setback standards.
9. The Applicant explained that a single -family house , with a limited exterior side
setback, had been located on the property and was demolished approximately 20 years ago.
10. The Applicant acknowledged that it would be feasible to construct a single -family
house on the Subject Property, but asserted that it would be more profitable to build a structure
for use for two residential units.
11. The Applicant identified other two-unit residential uses in the proximate area of the
Subject Property.
12. Peter Albrethson, the owner of the property at 1600 Westfield Street, appeared at
the hearing to support the request.
13. No one appeared at the hearing to oppose the request.
Conclusions of Law:
1. Pursuant to Section 911.02, two-unit residential uses are not permitted in R1D-H
Districts.
2. The site development standards for R1D-H Districts, as set forth in Section
903.03.D.2, include a 15’ exterior side setback requirement.
3. Pennsylvania law recognizes two distinct types of variances – use variances and
dimensional variances.  As the Pennsylvania Supreme Court explained in Hertzberg v. Zoning
Bd. of Adj. of the City of Pittsburgh, 721 A.2d 43, 47 (Pa. 1998), a “use variance” is a request to
use property in a manner that is outside the zoning regulations.  A dimensional variance, by
contrast, is a request for reasonable adjustment of the ordinance’s dimensional regulations, to
accommodate a use that is allowed in the relevant zoning district.  Id.
4. Section 922.09 of the Code sets forth the familiar standards that apply to all
requests for variances.  These include: (1) whether unique circumstances or conditions of a
property would result in in an unnecessary hardship for development; (2) whether the property
can be developed in strict conformity with the provisions of the Code; (3) whether the proposed
variance would have an adverse effect on the public welfare; and (4) whether the proposed
variance is the minimum variance that would afford relief with the least modification possible.
Marshall v. City of Philadelphia and Zoning Bd. of Adj., 97 A.3d 323, 329 (Pa. 2014); see also

3

Paganico v. Zoning Hearing Bd. of Penn Hills, 227 A.3d 949 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2020), quoting
Marshall.
5. Because dimensional variances require only a reasonable adjustment of the
zoning regulations to accommodate a use that is permitted in the relevant zoning district, a less
restrictive application of the standards is appropriate when considering requests for dimensional
variances.  The applicant for a dimensional variance still bears the burden of presenting
substantial and credible evidence regarding the variance standards.  See Hertzberg, 721 A.2d
at 47-48).
6. A variance cannot be granted simply because the zoning ordinance “deprives the
landowner of the most lucrative and profitable uses.”  Wilson v. Plumstead Twp. Zoning Hearing
Bd., 936 A.2d 1061, 1070 (Pa. 2007), citing Laurel Point Assoc. v. Susquehanna Twp. Zoning
Hearing Bd., 887 A.2d 796, 803 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2005), alloc. denied 903 A.2d 1235 (Pa.
2006); Valley View, 462 A.2d at 640 (citations omitted) (“mere evidence that the zoned use is
less financially rewarding than the proposed use is insufficient to justify a variance”);
7. Where no hardship is shown, or where the asserted hardship amounts to a simply
a desire to increase profitability, the “unnecessary hardship” standard required to obtain a
variance is not satisfied even under the relaxed Hertzberg standard for dimensional variances.
See Lamar Advantage GP Co. v. Zoning Hearing Bd. of Adj. of City of Pittsburgh, 997 A.2d 423,
445–46 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2010); Lawrenceville Stakeholders v. City of Pittsburgh Zoning Bd. of
Adj., 247 A.3d 465, 477 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2021).
8. The Applicant presented credible evidence that the width and topography of the
site are unique conditions that preclude strict compliance with the Code’s exterior side setback
requirement, and that the proposed 5’ exterior side setback would not have a significant impact
on the surrounding neighborhood.
9. The Applicant did not provide substantial or credible evidence of any unique
conditions that have the effect of preventing use of the property for a permitted purpose. The
Subject Property was previously used for a single-family house and the Applicant acknowledged
that it would be possible to use the property for a single-family house.
10.  A single-unit use might be less financially rewarding than the proposed two-unit
use but the Applicant did not demonstrate that the parcel could not be used for a single-unit use,
as permitted under the Code.
11. Consistent with the evidence and testimony, and the applicable legal standards
governing variances, the Board concludes that denial of the request for a use variance to allow
a two-unit use and approval of the request for a dimensional variance to allow a 5’ exterior side
setback is appropriate.
Decision: The Applicant’s request for a use variance from Section 911.02 to allow a two-
unit residential use on the Subject Property is hereby DENIED and the request
for a dimensional variance from the exterior side setback requirement in
Section 903.03.D.2 to allow a 5’ exterior side setback for the proposed
structure is hereby APPROVED.
