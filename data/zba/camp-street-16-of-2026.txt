Findings of Fact: 
1. The Subject Property is located at 3315 Camp Street, at the intersection with Burton 
Way, in an R2-L (Residential Two Unit Low Density) District in the Upper Hill neighborhood. 
2. The dimensions of the parcel are 55’ by 65’ (3,575 sf) and it is currently vacant. 
3. The grade of the parcel has a significant downward slope from the Clarissa Street 
side towards Burton Way. 
4. The Applicant proposes to construct a single -story house on the Subject Property 
that would be oriented towards Camp Street.  With that orientation, the sides of the Subject 
Property would be on Burton Way and along the rear of the parcel at 838 Clarissa S treet.  The 
rear property line of the Subject Property would be shared with the side property line of the parcel 
at 834 Clarissa Street. 
5. As proposed, the house on the Subject Property would be set back 8’ from the front 
property line; 20’ from the interior  side property line on Burton Way ; 6’-8” from the interior side 
 
2 
 
property line shared with the rear property line of the 838 Clarissa Street parcel; and 6’-11” from 
the rear property line shared with the interior side property line of the 834 Clarissa Street parcel. 
6. The Applicant indicated that the depth  and topography  of the parcel are unique 
conditions that preclude strict compliance with all of the Code’s setback standards. 
7. The Applicant explained that the house is  designed to include f eatures for 
accessibility. 
8. The Applicant provided photographs of other structures in the proximate area of the 
Subject Property with limited setbacks, and asserted that the reduced setbacks proposed for the 
house on the Subject Property will not have a significant impact on the surrounding neighborhood. 
9. No one appeared at the hearing to oppose the request. 
Conclusions of Law: 
1. The site development standards in Section 903.03.B.2 include 30’ front and 30’ 
rear setback requirements. 
2. The Applicant seeks dimensional variances to construct a house with an 8’ front 
and 6’-11” rear setback. 
3. The Applicant provided credible evidence that the depth and topography of the 
parcel are unique conditions that preclude strict compliance with the Code’s setback standards, 
and that the setbacks proposed for the house would not have a significant impact on the 
surrounding neighborhood. 
4. Consistent with the evidence and testimony presented, and the applicable legal 
standards governing dimensional variances, the Board concludes that approval of the request is 
appropriate. 
Decision: The Applicant’s request for variances from Section 903.03.B.2 to allow the 
construction of a house with limited front and rear setbacks is hereby 
APPROVED.
